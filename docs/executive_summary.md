# Executive Summary

*Independent analysis using publicly available data. Not affiliated with McKinsey & Company, Boston Consulting Group or Bain & Company.*

## The question
How have McKinsey, BCG and Bain positioned themselves competitively, and what differentiates their growth, geographic presence, industry exposure, capabilities and strategic priorities?

## The short answer
Public data supports a clear **relative profile** of each firm, a **measured but limited** view of growth, and **no direct view** of industry mix. The evidence is strongest for US hiring behavior and weakest for global revenue.

| | McKinsey | BCG | Bain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scale, 2023 (revenue / headcount) | about $16B / about 45K *(estimated)* | $12.3B / 32K *(reported)* | about $6B / about 19K *(estimated, low confidence)* |
| US H-1B filing growth, FY2016 to FY2024 | 12.5% a year | 10.4% a year | 2.2% a year |
| US footprint (states / cities) | 45 / 155 | 35 / 57 | 19 / 32 |
| Technical share of filings, FY2021 to FY2025 | 38.0% | 24.0% | 8.8% |
| Federal prime contracts, FY2015 to FY2024 | $762M, 19 agencies, 53% Defense | $826M, 10 agencies, 68% Defense | None found |
| Australian federal contract notices, 2015-2025 | 15, AUD 21M | 195, AUD 284M | None found |

## Key findings
1. **McKinsey has the broadest and most technical US profile.** It leads on footprint, filing growth and technical share, and sells to the widest set of federal agencies ([Sets 1, 2, 4](findings/)).
2. **BCG is the most transparent and shows steady growth.** Its reported revenue grew 9.8% a year from 2018 to 2025 and its technical share rose the most (+9.5 percentage points) ([Sets 3, 4](findings/)).
3. **BCG's federal work is more concentrated and has fallen sharply.** Defense is 68% of its contract value, and annual value fell from $217M (FY2020) to $1.5M (FY2024) ([Set 2](findings/02_public_sector_exposure.md)).
4. **Bain is smaller, more concentrated and least technical,** with slow US filing growth (2.2% a year) and no federal prime contracts in the data ([Sets 1, 2, 4](findings/)).
5. **The firms cannot be compared on revenue over the decade from public sources.** Only BCG publishes; McKinsey and Bain figures are estimates and begin around 2018 to 2023 ([Set 3](findings/03_firm_scale_and_reach.md)).

## What the evidence supports, and how strongly
| Hypothesis | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| McKinsey grew its US hiring faster than BCG, and BCG faster than Bain | Supported, descriptively | Filing CAGRs 12.5%, 10.4%, 2.2%. No significance test was run, and filing practice differs by firm |
| McKinsey is the largest of the three | Supported, with caveats | Estimates for McKinsey, reported for BCG |
| All three have shifted toward technical and analytical capabilities | Supported | Technical share rose at each firm between FY2015-19 and FY2021-25 |
| BCG and McKinsey are similarly exposed to the US federal government | Partly supported | Similar total value, but different agency mix and BCG's recent decline |
| Industry exposure differs across the firms | **Not testable** | No public industry revenue split exists |

## Implications
These follow from the findings; they are suggestions, not proven causal claims.
1. **Treat BCG as the reference firm for any quantitative benchmark,** because it is the only one with reported, continuous figures. McKinsey and Bain comparisons should always carry an "estimated" label.
2. **Do not read H-1B filings as headcount.** Cross-firm gaps there mix hiring scale with filing practice (McKinsey files many positions per application).
3. **Read the technical-share trend as the clearest strategic signal in the data.** All three moved the same way, at different speeds and from different starting points.
4. **For a fuller industry view, different data is needed:** client-side contract disclosures, job postings by industry, or the firms' own publication metadata, each of which would need its own terms-of-use and quality review.

## Limitations of the whole study
- The H-1B and federal-contract sources describe the **US only**.
- Several scale figures for McKinsey and Bain are secondary-source estimates that were not independently verified.
- No statistical significance tests were run; findings are descriptive.
- Industry mix and strategic priorities are not measured.
